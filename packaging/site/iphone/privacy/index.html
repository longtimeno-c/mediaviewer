Privacy

MediaViewer for NAS, for iPhone and iPad. Last changed 12 November 2026.

The app collects no data. There is no account to make, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no crash reports sent anywhere. Nothing you do in the app, and nothing about your photos and videos, is sent to Oakforge Studios LTD, the app's publisher, or to anyone else. We run no server for this app.

Who the app talks to

Only the MediaViewer service on your own NAS, at the addresses in the code you scanned from MediaViewer on your computer: its address on its own network and, if you set one up, its name on the internet. Every connection is encrypted end to end, and the app checks that the service is the one it paired with before it asks for anything. The service only lets in phones you paired, and only reads the folders you chose to share with them.

Over that connection the app copies your library's catalogue and fetches thumbnails, photos and clips as you look at them. It never writes to your NAS.

During setup, on a phone with mobile data, the app tries once to reach your NAS's internet name over mobile data, so it can tell you whether the app will work away from home. Looking up that name goes through your network's or carrier's name service, as any app's connection does.

Permissions

  • Camera. Only to scan the code MediaViewer shows on your computer. The picture is read on the phone to find the code; it is not saved or sent.
  • Local network. To reach your NAS on the network you are on.
  • Photos, add only. Asked for the first time you tap Save to Photos, and used only to add the copy you asked for. Add-only access cannot see or read anything already in your Photos library.

You can turn any of these off in the Settings app. Without the camera you cannot pair a new library; without Photos you cannot save copies; everything else keeps working.

What is kept on your phone

  • The catalogue of your library, copied from your NAS so that search works without a connection: file names, folders, dates, places, camera details, the names of people you named in MediaViewer on your computer, words found in pictures and words said in clips, small thumbnails, and the model the app searches with.
  • A cache of thumbnails, photos and clips you opened, so they show again quickly. iOS may clear it when the phone needs space.
  • Your recent searches (the last 20), which you can remove one by one or clear on the search screen.
  • The pairing keys that let the phone into your NAS, in the iOS Keychain, kept on this device only.
  • The app's settings, such as its appearance.

The catalogue, the cache and your recent searches are kept out of iCloud and computer backups, so a copy of your library's catalogue does not leave the phone that way. They are protected by iOS's data protection, locked until you first unlock the phone after it starts.

Removing it

Settings → Forget this library removes everything the app keeps about the library: the catalogue, the cache, your recent searches and the pairing keys. Nothing on your NAS changes. You can also remove the phone from MediaViewer on your computer, after which the service on your NAS no longer lets it in. Deleting the app removes its files with it.

Children

The app is not aimed at children and collects nothing from anyone.

Changes

If this ever changes, this page changes first, with a new date at the top. The app's source code is public, so anyone can check what it does.

Contact

Questions about privacy: open an issue at github.com/longtimeno-c/mediaviewer/issues. Issues are public, so please leave out anything personal.
