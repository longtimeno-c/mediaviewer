MediaViewer for NAS

Your NAS library, searched from your phone. Support for the iPhone and iPad app.

What you need

The app shows a library that MediaViewer on your computer has already indexed. On its own it has nothing to show. You need:

  • MediaViewer on a Mac or Windows PC, with Local search and Driftwire installed, and your library on the NAS indexed by Local search. MediaViewer is free: download it here.
  • A Synology NAS running the MediaViewer service, which serves the library to your phone. Your computer claims the service and adds phones to it.
  • To use it away from home: a public IPv4 address with one port forwarded to the service, or a public IPv6 address with that port open. Without either, the app works only on the NAS's own network. Forward that one port only: never the NAS's file sharing or admin page.

Your computer does not need to be on once the phone is paired. Search runs on the phone, so it works with no connection at all; photos and clips come from the NAS.

Pairing a phone

  1. In MediaViewer on your computer, open Settings → Local search → Phone access. Claim the service on your NAS once, from the NAS's own network.
  2. Choose Add a phone → Show code. A code appears, with your library's name and a check code.
  3. In the app, tap Scan code and point the camera at it. No camera? Choose a picture of the code, or paste the link MediaViewer shows next to it.
  4. Check that the phone shows the same library name and check code as the computer, and pair only if they match.
  5. The app connects to the NAS, tests the route from outside over mobile data (an iPad without mobile data skips this and says so), and copies the catalogue. A large library can be hundreds of megabytes: the app shows the size first, and waits for Wi-Fi unless you tap to copy now.

The code works once and expires after a short time. If it expires, show a new one.

Common problems

“Can't reach your Synology.”

The app lists each address it tried and why it failed. Check that the NAS is on and the MediaViewer service is running, and, away from home, that the port is still forwarded. You can keep searching what is on the phone meanwhile, and tap Try again in Settings.

“Your Synology can't be reached from the internet.”

The phone reached the NAS at home but not from outside. Your provider may not give that building a public address (many share one address between customers), or the port is not forwarded. Ask your provider for a public IPv4 address, or use IPv6 if your provider offers it.

“This code has expired.” or “That isn't a code MediaViewer can read.”

Show a new code in MediaViewer on your computer and scan that one. The app only reads codes from MediaViewer itself.

“Last updated … days ago.”

The phone has the last catalogue your computer published. Open MediaViewer on your computer with the NAS reachable so it publishes a new one; the phone picks it up the next time it connects.

“This one hasn't reached your Synology yet.”

The catalogue knows the item but the file is not on the NAS yet, for example straight after an import. Try again once it has copied.

“Your Synology isn't sharing the folder this came from.”

Add that folder to the service in MediaViewer on your computer, named the same as its shared folder on the NAS.

“The MediaViewer service on your Synology needs updating.”

The app is newer than the service on the NAS. Update the service, then open the app again.

“This phone was removed from your library on your computer.”

Someone removed this phone in Phone access. Scan a new code to use it again.

“This library was published from a different computer.”

The library's signing key changed without the old computer handing it over. Scan a new code from the computer that publishes the library now.

“This library uses the largest search model, which this phone can't run yet.”

Local search on your computer uses its largest model. Choose a smaller one there to search from the phone.

“MediaViewer isn't allowed to add to Photos.”

Turn on Photos for MediaViewer in the Settings app. The app asks only to add photos, never to read your Photos library.

Starting again

Settings → Forget this library removes everything the app keeps on the phone. Nothing on your NAS changes. Scan a new code to connect again.

Still stuck?

Open an issue at github.com/longtimeno-c/mediaviewer/issues with your iPhone or iPad model, the app's version (Settings → About), and the message the app showed. Issues are public, so leave out addresses, file names and anything personal.
